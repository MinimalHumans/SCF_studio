## One thing to look at

The props scorer was tuned against Frankenstein, and the swap exposed it.
Its medium ("strong") tier on Alexis Nexus is:

    Door (score 8), Closet Door (7), Charts (6), Portfolio (6)

Charts and Portfolio are right — both are introduced, handled and recur
across scenes, and both drive sequences. Door and Closet Door are set
dressing: "door" appears 119 times across 45 locations, and the scorer
reads recurrence + handling with no notion of whether an object travels
with the story or just exists in every room.

The old test asserted named props (`Rifle`, `Umbrella`, `Blunderbuss`)
and named noise (`Sun`, `Men`, `Howling`). Neither list transfers, and I
didn't want to bless whatever came out as if it were correct. So the
block now asserts:

- Portfolio and Charts are in the strong tier — hand-verified, not a
  baseline
- the strong tier stays under 10 entries
- verb fragments stay out of it (`Breathe`, `Bite`, `Door Shut`,
  `Eyes Within` — all present at *low* confidence, so this has teeth)
- ordering is strongest-first
- **and one test named "set dressing currently reaches the strong tier
  (documented gap)" that asserts `Door` is there**

That last one is deliberately a regression baseline in the sense your
corpus README already defines: it records what the classifier does, not
what you want. It will fail the day you add a distinctness or
location-spread signal, which is the point — flip it to `not.toContain`
then. If you'd rather not carry a known-bad assertion in a green suite,
`test.fails()` or `test.todo()` both work; I left it as a passing test so
`npm test` stays a clean signal.
